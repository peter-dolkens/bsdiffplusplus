# What has been learned since 2006 that is worth adding to bsdiff 6

Research report for the bsdiff6 C# project. Question: are there post-2006 methods that give a
better compression ratio, or lower resource use at the same ratio, and which are worth adding?
Everything here was measured on the project's own corpus (`bench/corpus`, 20 old/new pairs)
unless a line says "estimate" or "literature". How to rerun everything: [`README.md`](README.md).
This file is generated by `tools/build_report.py` from `report_src/main.tmpl` and the tables
that `tools/tables.py` computes from `results/*.json`.

**Conventions.** Averages are the benchmark's: mean of size/new weighted by sqrt(new size), per
category (`upgrade` 10 pairs, `patch` 8, `other` 2). Pair names are shortened (`u:` upgrade,
`p:` patch, `o:` other). "Verified" means the prototype encoded a real stream, decoded it again
without access to the new file, and compared. "Code length" means the exact cost of an adaptive
model or of a uniform arithmetic code, not an estimate.

**The alignment used.** Unless a table says otherwise, experiments use the exact alignment
recovered from real bsdiff 4.3 patches (which bytes are copied-with-diff from where, which are
extra), so that differences between columns are due to the encoding only. Sections 6.2 and 9.2
repeat the main results on the project's own DP alignment, from a snapshot copy of its sources.

## 0. Summary

### 0.1 The byte budget

In bsdiff 4.3 patches the extra string is 52% of the bytes, the diff string 34%, the control
string 14% (sqrt-weighted). The two main categories are opposites: `upgrade` patches are 53%
extra, `patch` (small source change) patches are 66% diff. So the extra-string codec decides
the `upgrade` number, and the coding of matched bytes decides the `patch` number. The control
string is not negligible: 10–19% of the patch on the fragmented large-change pairs.

### 0.2 Ranked recommendations

Sizes are sqrt-weighted patch/new on all 20 pairs, on the same alignment (bsdiff 4.3's), so the
differences are due to the encoding only. bsdiff 4.3 itself: 0.1518 (upgrade 0.1978, patch
0.0483). The thesis format as designed (DESIGN §7) on that alignment: 0.1399 (−7.8%).

**On the project's own DP alignment** (snapshot, 20 pairs, verified): thesis format 0.1330;
P1+P2 stream with control records and extra bytes in the model 0.1036, i.e. **−22% against the
thesis format on the same alignment** and −32% against bsdiff 4.3 (`patch` −53%, `upgrade`
−31%). The gains carry over (section 9.2).

If only one thing is done, do **P3** (an afternoon, no new dependency, −11% on the thesis
format's patches). If the `patch` category (security-update-like changes) is what matters, **P1+P2**
halve those patches and nothing else comes close.

**Not worth it** (numbers in section 8.2): BCJ filters on the extra string; branch-convert-then-
diff; zstd as a back end; a context-mixing coder for the *extra* string (the prototype's is
worse than LZMA2 and 20–100× slower to decode); keeping four difference modes once P1 is in;
disassembly; FFT block alignment *in addition to* local alignment + DP; ANS.

### 0.3 Surprises, and where the evidence contradicts the brief or DESIGN.md

1. **Direct conditional modelling beats BWT + midpoint for the difference map by 59%, on the
   format agent's own inputs** (175,041 → 72,166 bytes, real coder, verified; section 5.1). The
   earlier finding that direct models lose by 10–25% holds for models that see previous map
   bits and one old byte. What changes the picture is conditioning on the old file's bytes
   *around* the position (including the bytes after it, which the decoder has) and on the
   values of recent non-zero digits, with learned mixing.
2. **The four difference modes are unnecessary** with a conditional coder: little-endian digits
   were best or tied on every pair tried, including pairs where the thesis coder picks
   "correction" (section 3.2).
3. **DESIGN 7.6's codec menu is the wrong one for the extra string.** zlib beats bzip2 on 16 of
   the 17 extra strings over 1 kB, and LZMA2 or Brotli beat both on all 17 (section 2.1).
4. **Plain LZ77 with the old file as dictionary is a serious competitor on large-change pairs.**
   Raw LZMA2 with `old` as preset dictionary, no executable knowledge at all: −27% against bsdiff
   4.3 on the upgrade category (−23…−37% on the six large-change pairs, +12…+54% on the four
   small-change ones), close to P1+P2+P3 on bsdiff 4.3's alignment there (−28…−30%); on `patch`
   pairs it is +1.5% where P1+P2+P3 is −50% (sections 2.4, 3.3). bsdiff's weak
   spot on large-change pairs is the isolated extra string plus a control string of tens of
   thousands of records.
5. **The 25–35% patches of the Rust and Go pairs are mostly genuinely new code, caused by
   toolchain changes.** Every large Rust pair crosses a rustc version, and the big Go pair goes
   from Go 1.20 to 1.22; 25–59% of `.text` is unmatched. The Go pair built with nearly the same
   compiler patches at 5.5%, and *there* the remaining cost is predictable structure: `pclntab`
   and type-link tables of base-relative offsets (section 4.5).
6. **The file-offset vs virtual-address problem is smaller than feared.** With no container
   knowledge at all (VA = file offset) the matched-data cost is −42.0% against the thesis
   coding; with the container's address map it is −42.6%. The section table matters on PE (−20% on jq windows) and for ELF data segments
   (−5…−11%), not at all on Mach-O. It fits in the patch header, so the apply side needs no
   format parser (section 4.3).
7. **Zucchini has nothing for AArch64 ADRP/ADD pairs or literal loads, and nothing for Mach-O**
   (section 7.2); here ADRP pairs and their low-12 halves are 10–33% of the differing bytes on
   the small-change arm64 pairs.
8. **bsdiff 6 was reimplemented in Rust and measured in 2026**: about −5% against bsdiff 4
   with bzip2 packaging, attributed to the DP, not to block alignment (section 7.1). That
   matches section 6.2 here: the DP alignment is worth −4.9% against bsdiff 4.3's under the
   thesis coding, and adding block alignment to it changes the patch size by −0.1%.
9. **Converting relative calls to absolute before diffing makes `patch` pairs worse** (+6%;
   section 4.6).

### 0.4 What was not done

Section 9.3. The main ones: no fixed-point (portable) version of the model was built, so a C#
port must be re-measured; speeds were taken on a shared, throttled machine; the alignment was
not re-optimised for the new coder; P5 is measured on one pair and P6 is an oracle result.

Readings:

- **`patch` pairs: 97–100% of the new file is matched, 0.4–4.9% of matched bytes differ, and the
  diff string is 50–76% of the patch.** This is the thesis's "security update" case; it is

- **`upgrade` pairs split in two.** Four are small-change (jq 1.7→1.7.1 on two platforms, fzf,

  patch/new 17–34%); only 52–88% of the new file is matched and the extra string is 41–68% of
  the patch.
- **Control is 4–19% of the patch on the executable pairs.** On the large-change pairs bsdiff
  4.3 emits 36,000–195,000 control records for a 3.6–19 MB file: a copy every 30–100 bytes, most of them a 7–30 byte
  fragment found somewhere far away (section 6.2).
- **`other` (text) pairs** are tiny patches dominated by the control string and by genuinely
  new text.

## 2. Back-end codecs

Method (`tools/q2_codecs.py`): each raw section of each patch was compressed with each codec at
its strongest setting; every size is a real stream (Python `zlib`/`bz2`/`lzma` for raw LZMA2,
the `brotli` 1.2.0 and `zstd` 1.5.7 CLIs, `xz` 5.8.4 for the ARM64 BCJ chain, `tools/lzdict.c`
on liblzma for LZMA2 with a preset dictionary, round-trip checked). Columns give the
sqrt(raw size)-weighted mean of compressed/raw and the change against the codec the design
currently prefers for that section. The first run hit a time limit on the shared machine; the
"pairs" column says how many pairs a row covers (the `lp` tuning and BCJ variants cover the 16
pairs up to 5.8 MB and were not repeated for the four largest; everything else covers all 20,
the extra strings of sqlite3.c, bzip2 and libz.a are under 1 kB).

- **LZMA2 is 19% and Brotli (at the BCL's limits: quality 11, 16 MiB window) 18% smaller than
  bzip2; zstd −22 is 13% smaller.** LZMA2 is the best single codec on 15 of the 17 extra
  strings over 1 kB, Brotli on 2, bzip2 and zlib on none. Between the two codecs DESIGN 7.6
  offers, zlib beats bzip2 on 16 of 17 (bzip2 wins on `fd`, by 1%): the thesis's rule "extra compresses best with bzip2" does not hold for these
  binaries.
- **BCJ filters do nothing here** (x86: ±0.1%; ARM64: 1% *worse*). The extra string is a
  concatenation of fragments; a filter that turns relative targets into absolute ones using the
  position in the stream produces inconsistent values. LZMA literal-position tuning (`lp`,
  `pb`) is worth nothing either (±0.5%).
- **The old file as dictionary is worth another 3–4% of the extra string** on large-change
  pairs and 5–12% on small ones (LZMA2 preset dictionary: −22% against bzip2; Brotli `-D`:
  −21%; zstd `--patch-from`: −16%). So there is redundancy between "new"
  code and old code that the alignment leaves on the table, but it is modest: the alignment
  already took the long matches.

## 3. Conditional modelling instead of subtract-then-compress

### 3.1 The prototype

`tools/cm.c` codes the new file as one adaptive binary arithmetic-coded stream in new-file
order. For a matched byte it codes a **flag** ("the difference digit is non-zero", i.e. the map
bit) and, if set, the eight bits of the **digit** (i.e. the non-zero string). The digit is the
thesis's little-endian balanced digit with the carry handled exactly as in
`DifferenceCoder.cs`, so the information coded is identical to the thesis format's mode 2; only
the modelling differs. For an extra byte it codes eight bits.

Each binary decision is predicted lpaq-style: a set of contexts each select an adaptive
probability in a hashed table; the probabilities are combined in the logistic domain by three
small online-trained mixers whose weight sets are selected by further contexts, and a final
mixer combines those. All contexts are built from what the decoder already has:

- **flag**: the last 16 flags; the old byte at this position with its neighbours on both sides
  (`old[i-3 .. i+3]`: the decoder has the whole old file, so it can look *ahead*); position
  mod 4; the flags 4, 8, 12, 16 positions back; the last non-zero digit and the distance since
  it; the digits 1 and 4 positions back and the carry; the distance from the segment ends; and
  four "what was the digit the last time these four old bytes were seen" memories (the
  old-window memories: the same old address tends to become the same new address);
- **digit value**: the old byte and its neighbours, the last one to three non-zero digits, the
  digits at distance 1, 2 and 4, the last non-zero digit at the same position mod 4, the
  old-window memories, and a match model over the history of non-zero digits;
- **extra byte**: orders 1–4 and 6 of the preceding new bytes, three sparse contexts for
  4-byte instruction sets, a match model over the old file plus everything decoded so far, and
  the old byte that would have continued the previous matched segment.

The address hypotheses of section 4 enter as additional inputs. The arithmetic coder is a
64-bit carry-less binary coder with 16-bit probabilities (12 bits are not enough: 9 MB of
unchanged text costs 86 bytes of flags here and would cost about 400 at 12 bits).

Every size below is the length of a real stream that the same program decoded back and
compared with `new`: all 20 pairs in all three configurations of section 3.2.

## 4. Alignment-driven address prediction

### 4.1 Idea and mechanism

A second-order change is an address that moved. Given the alignment, the patcher knows where
every old byte went, so for a field of the old file that is some kind of reference it can
compute what the field should be in the new file: read the old target, map the target through
the alignment, re-encode it relative to the field's new location. `tools/addr.h` does this with
no disassembly and no container parsing on the apply side:

- `FWD[x]`: old offset → new offset, from the segments (longest segment wins where old bytes
  are used twice), **extrapolated across unmatched gaps** from the nearest matched neighbour, so
  that a call into a function whose body was rewritten still gets a prediction.
- **Hypotheses**, each a pure function of the old file, the alignment and the position:

  | hypothesis | field | target | where tried |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `rel32` | 4 bytes LE | field end + value (+1 or +4 when a 2-byte x86 opcode rule says an immediate follows) | every byte offset (4 placements per byte) |
  | `selfrel32` | 4 bytes LE | field start + value (DWARF `pcrel`, relative tables) | 4-aligned |
  | `abs64` | 8 bytes LE | the value is an address | 8-aligned and 4-mod-8 |
  | `abs32`, `rva32` | 4 bytes LE | the value, or image base + value | 4-aligned |
  | ARM64 `B`/`BL` | imm26 | pc + 4·imm | 4-aligned word with the opcode bits |
  | ARM64 `ADRP` + the following `ADD`/`LDR`/`STR` on the same register | imm21 page, imm12 | page + low 12 bits mapped as one address; both instructions predicted | as above |
  | ARM64 imm19 (`B.cond`, `CBZ`, `LDR` literal) | imm19 | pc + 4·imm | as above |

- A hypothesis decides nothing. Its predicted byte is given to the model as an **expected value
  with a learned reliability**. The reliability context is: which hypothesis; the old byte
  before the field (for `rel32` this is how the model discovers that `E8`, `E9`, `0F 8x` and
  RIP-relative ModRM bytes introduce real fields, without being told); whether the prediction
  differs from the old field; and whether the bytes of this field that are already decoded
  agreed with it. So "telling the decoder which hypothesis applies where" costs exactly what
  the adaptive model charges, and that cost is inside every size reported.

**File offsets vs virtual addresses.** Targets are computed in virtual addresses through a list
of regions (file offset range → VA) for the old and for the new file, plus an image base. With
no list, one identity region is used (VA = file offset): the fully format-agnostic mode
("identity map"). The "container map" columns use the regions that `tools/vamap.py` reads from
ELF program headers, Mach-O segments or PE section headers. The intended design ships the two
small tables in the patch header: the *diff* side may parse containers (or infer the
constants, or leave them empty) and the *apply* side stays a format-agnostic interpreter.
Addresses beyond the file-backed part of a region (`.bss`) shift with the end of that region.

### 4.4 What Zucchini does that this misses, and the reverse

From the Zucchini sources (survey; details in section 7.2):

- Zucchini matches on a token view in which reference bodies are replaced by type and label,
  so the *alignment* is found through changed references, and it iterates map ↔ labels. Here
  the alignment is found on raw bytes with mismatches tolerated. Whether reference-aware
  matching would change which regions get matched on large-change pairs was not tested.
- Zucchini sends each target that lies in inserted code once per pool and codes references to
  it as small index deltas. Here a reference whose target the map gets wrong is paid for by the
  generic model (2–4 bytes). The learned-target oracle of section 4.2 bounds what is lost: 1–11%
  of differing bytes on large-change pairs.
- Zucchini knows where references are from a disassembler and the relocation table (`abs32`
  only at reloc entries), so it has no false positives. Here false positives are harmless
  because predictions are only model inputs.
- The reverse: Zucchini has no ADRP+ADD/LDR pairs and no literal loads on AArch64, no pointers
  outside relocation tables, no entropy coder of its own, and nothing for containers it cannot
  parse (PE, ELF and DEX only: no Mach-O).

Findings:

- **Toolchain changes.** Embedded version strings show that every large Rust pair is built by
  a different rustc (`fd`: 1.80.1 → 1.89.0; ripgrep linux: 1.76 → 1.77 and at the same time

  matched to anything and 6–11% of the matched part differs: the compiler regenerated the code.
  That is first-order change in the thesis's terms and no address model recovers it. In the
  Rust pairs the new code is where the bytes go: `.text` is 79% of the stream for ripgrep
  windows and 52% for bat, read-only constants most of the rest. By contrast `fzf` (go1.20.13
  on both sides) and `lazygit` 0.43.1 → 0.44.0 (go1.22.5 → go1.22.6) patch at 2% and 5.5%.

### 5.2 Apply-side resources

| | bsdiff 4.3 | thesis format (as implemented) | conditional stream |
|---|---|---|---|
| order of output | streaming, new-file order | whole map decoded first; big-endian mode walks each record backwards | streaming, new-file order |
| memory beyond the old file and the output | three bzip2 decoders (about 4 MB each) | D/8 + D/16 bytes working memory for the inverse BWT, the map itself (D bytes, or D/8 bit-packed), the decompressed non-zero string | model tables (fixed: 4–64 MB by configuration, section 3.4); for address hypotheses the segment list (12 bytes per control record) with binary search, or 4 bytes per old byte as a flat array |
| random access needed | old file | old file | old file |
| CPU | fast | fast (inverse BWT + LZ decode) | symmetric: equal to the encoder's coding cost |
| encode-side extras | — | one suffix sort of the D-symbol map per candidate mode (four) | none |

With the corrected inverse-BWT memory (D/8 + D/16 bytes), memory is no argument against the
BWT map. What remains: it is not streaming, it costs four suffix sorts at encode time, and it
is 2.4–2.9× larger than the conditional alternative.

**A patch format that applies in O(1) memory beyond the old file** is the conditional stream
with the control records front-loaded (they are small), the extra string as its own LZ stream
with a bounded window (Brotli at most 16 MiB), and the address map evaluated by binary search
over the segment list. It costs nothing in ratio: it is the best-compressing variant measured
here. The price is apply CPU (section 3.4).

### 5.3 A drop-in middle step

The flag model alone can replace the BWT + midpoint coder as `mapModel = 1` in the existing
layout: the non-zero string is stored before the map, so the map decoder can consume digits as
it decodes flags and use them as context. That keeps the non-zero string in zlib/Brotli and
takes the −59% on the map only: on the 14-pair set, map + non-zero would go from 650,737 to
about 548,000 bytes (−16%; a sum of two measured columns) instead of 403,567 (−38%).

### 6.1 Alternatives for the alignment stage (analysis)

  are small.

  coding** (upgrade −5.1%, patch −2.6%, text +4.5%), which is the size of effect the published
  reimplementation reports (section 7.1). It is better on 14 pairs and worse on 6.
- **FFT block alignment adds nothing measurable on top of local alignment + DP on this
  corpus.** On the 18 pairs the snapshot aligner completed in both modes, the thesis-format
  patch changes by −0.1% on average (−0.3% on `patch` pairs; table above). "Local" and "local +
  block" differ by fewer than 300 matched bytes on 16 pairs and by 0.1% of matched bytes on the
  largest difference (jq windows: +1,046 of 974,506), and the segment counts differ by 0–10.
  The block aligner cost 3–4 CPU seconds per pair next to 8–40 s for the suffix sort. This
  agrees with the published reimplementation (section 7.1). Its place is the block-only,
  O(√n)-memory mode (+8% patch size by the thesis's own numbers); **block-only mode was not
  measured here.**
- **Is the FFT still the best tool for "mismatches every few bytes"?** For a low-memory mode,
  yes: nothing else in the table finds a mismatch-tolerant alignment in O(√n) memory. As an
  *addition* to a suffix array that is already in memory, no: the DP carries the current offset
  through such regions on its own as long as fewer than half the bytes mismatch, and the
  measurement says the remaining cases are too rare to matter. Spaced seeds would handle the
  address-table case more cheaply than the FFT, but there is no measured need for either.
- The DP alignment makes 2–7× fewer segments than bsdiff 4.3 (199,383 against 731,992 over the
  corpus; ripgrep windows 22,205 vs 149,038; bat 20,413 vs 63,069), and its control string is
  about a third of the size (section 9.2).

### 6.3 The alignment objective and the new coder

The DP's costs (mismatch 2, unmatched 1, switch 20) encode an assumption about what the coder
charges. Under the conditional coder with address hypotheses the real prices are different: a
mismatching byte inside a correctly predicted address field costs almost nothing, a mismatch in
changed code costs about a byte, an unmatched byte about half a byte, and a control record 2–4
bytes. Section 9.2 has the measurement: under the conditional coder the DP alignment is 5.8% better
than bsdiff 4.3's overall (0.1036 vs 0.1099, control records included), but it is *worse* on 6
of 20 pairs (lua x64, sqlite shell, jq linux, bzip2 and both text pairs). It spends more on
matched data on 14 of 20 pairs (jq windows: 8,630 vs 4,891 bytes; sqlite shell 11,926 vs
8,401) and gets that back, or not, through fewer control records and fewer extra bytes. Its
fixed prices do not know that a mismatch inside a predictable address field is nearly free
while a mismatch in changed code costs a byte.
A cost-aware second pass (re-run the DP with byte costs taken from the hypotheses computed on
the first alignment, the way Zucchini iterates map and labels) is the obvious next step and was
**not prototyped**.

### 7.3 Entropy coding

- **ANS** (rANS/tANS; zstd's FSE, LZFSE, JPEG XL): a faster replacement for static or
  semi-static arithmetic coding of multi-symbol alphabets. It does not help here: the gains
  measured in sections 3–5 come from *adaptive, context-conditioned binary* probabilities, and
  ANS decodes in reverse order of encoding, which is awkward for exactly that kind of model
  (adaptive binary coding with rANS needs the encoder to buffer and replay probabilities).
  A carry-less binary range coder is the right tool and is already in the project (`RangeCoder.cs`).
- **Context mixing** (lpaq/paq8/cmix/zpaq): the technique used in sections 3–5. The closest prior
  art for conditioning on the aligned old byte is LZMA's *matched literal* coding (after a match,
  the literal is coded bit by bit with the corresponding bit of the byte at `rep0` as context);
  with the old file as preset dictionary that is a (much weaker) form of the coder proposed here.
  Reference genome compressors (GeCo, GReEn) do the same with a copy model on a reference. No
  published general-purpose context-mixing *delta* coder for executables was found (*unverified
  absence*).

### 7.4 Theory after 2006

Exact k-mismatch: Clifford, Fontaine, Porat, Sach, Starikovskaya (SODA 2016),
O(n k² log k / m + n polylog m); Gawrychowski–Uznański (2018), Õ((m + k√m)·n/m). Approximate
text-to-pattern Hamming distance: Chan, Golan, Kociumaka, Kopelowitz, Porat (STOC 2020),
(1+ε)-approximation in O(ε⁻² n) by random sampling *without* FFT. Sparse FFT (Hassanieh, Indyk,
Katabi, Price 2012): O(k log n) for exactly k-sparse spectra. None of these changes the practical
picture: the thesis's method is already a randomised projection (mod primes) plus FFT with
O(m log n) total work, and its cost is not what limits this project (section 6). Whether any of
this line cites or supersedes the thesis's Chapter 1 is *unverified*.

### 7.5 Suffix sorting

libsais (Grebnov, 2021–): SA-IS, 4n bytes for the array plus ~16 KB, about 2.5× faster than
divsufsort single-threaded; no pure-managed port found on NuGet (`NLibSais` is a native binding).
Managed options that exist: `DeltaQ.SuffixSorting.SAIS` and `…LibDivSufSort` ("100% safe,
managed"), `SAIS-CSharp`. The project's own `SaIs.cs` is the right call; there is nothing to
take here beyond confirming that 4n (not 9n) is the state of the art for the array itself.
<https://github.com/IlyaGrebnov/libsais>, <https://github.com/jzebedee/deltaq>

## 8. Proposals

### 8.1 Ranked extensions, with format sketches

#### P3. Codec ids for the three strings (do this first)

- **Gain** (measured, real streams, 20 pairs): extra string −19% (Brotli) / −20% (LZMA2) /
  −22% (LZMA2 with the old file as preset dictionary) in total bytes against bzip2; non-zero
  −8…−9% and control −5…−8% against zlib. Thesis-format patch/new 0.1399 → 0.1247 with Brotli
  alone. Confidence: high.
- **Cost**: decodes faster than bzip2; 9–11 MB.
- **Size**: Brotli about 30 lines (BCL). LZMA2 needs a managed dependency (7-Zip's `LZMA-SDK`
  C#, or SharpCompress).
- **Platform-agnostic**: yes.
- **Format sketch**: the `modes` byte has two bits per string; give each string its own byte
  (version 2 header): `0 none, 1 zlib, 2 bzip2, 3 Brotli (quality 11, lgwin = clamp(ceil(log2
  len), 10, 24))`, `4 LZMA2 raw (lc 3, lp 0, pb 2; dictionary = length rounded up to a power of
  two)`, `5 LZMA2 raw with the old file as preset dictionary (extra string only)`. The encoder
  keeps trying all enabled ids and keeps the smallest, as now. Decoders must cap the declared
  window/dictionary (reader hardening).

#### P1. Conditional coder for matched bytes (`coder = 1`)

- **Gain** (measured, verified, 20 pairs, bsdiff 4.3 alignment): matched-data bytes −34%
  against the thesis coding on its own. Confidence: high for the direction and rough size; the
  C# port must use fixed-point mixing and will differ by a few percent (not measured).
- **Cost**: apply time equals the coding time on the diff side: about 0.5–2 µs per byte in the
  prototype on a slow core. Fixed model memory. The diff side loses the four suffix sorts of
  the map.
- **Size**: about 600 lines of C# (binary arithmetic coder, hashed probability tables, mixer,
  context functions).
- **Platform-agnostic**: yes.
- **Format sketch** (a new DESIGN section, used instead of 7.4–7.6 when `coder = 1`):

  ```
  header as 7.7 with version 2 and:
    coder       1 byte   0 = thesis coding (7.4–7.6), 1 = conditional stream
    tableLog2   1 byte   log2 of the probability table sizes (18..24)
  when coder = 1 the sizes line is
    ctrlCLen, ctrlLen, extraCLen, extraLen, streamLen      varints
  and the body is
    control string (ctrlCLen) ‖ conditional stream (streamLen) ‖ extra string (extraCLen)
  ```

  The control and extra strings are as in 7.2/7.3 with the codec ids of P3. The conditional
  stream is one binary arithmetic-coded bit sequence. The decoder walks the control records;
  for each record it sets the carry `c = 0` and for each of the `copyLen` positions decodes

  1. `z` (1 bit) with model F;
  2. if `z = 1`, the digit `d` as 8 bits, most significant first, with model V (the context
     includes the bits of `d` decoded so far);
  3. `new = UnDigit(old, z ? d : 0, ref c)` exactly as in 7.4 (little-endian digits only).

  Arithmetic coder: 64-bit `x1 = 0`, `x2 = 2^64−1`; `xmid = x1 + ((x2 − x1) >> 16) · p` with
  `p` = P(bit = 1) in 1..65535; bit 1 takes `[x1, xmid]`, bit 0 `[xmid+1, x2]`; while the top
  bytes of `x1` and `x2` are equal, shift that byte out; flush the top byte of `x2`; bytes past
  the end read as zero.

  Models F and V are fixed by the specification: the context list, the hash, the probability
  update and the mixer, all in integer arithmetic. The prototype's exact contexts are `cf[]`
  and `cv[]` in `tools/cm.c` (summarised in section 3.1). They are a starting point, not a
  frozen format: port to fixed point first, then re-run `tools/run_cm.py`.

  Reader hardening is simple: each decoded decision produces at most one output bit, table
  indices are masked, `streamLen` bounds the reads, and the control string is validated as now.

#### P2. Address hypotheses (requires P1)

- **Gain** (measured, verified, 20 pairs): matched-data bytes −43% against the thesis coding
  together with P1 (−58% on `patch` pairs); over P1 alone −13% on average, −33% on `patch`
  pairs, −40…−56% on small-change executables, −7…−12% on large-change upgrades, nothing on
  text. Confidence: high.
- **Cost**: +30–60% coding time in the prototype; the old→new map.
- **Size**: about 400 lines (the map, seven field shapes, the reliability contexts).
- **Platform-agnostic**: the mechanism is; the shapes are little-endian x86-64 and AArch64
  flavoured. On other data the hypotheses do not fire or are learned to be unreliable (measured
  cost on the text pairs: 1 byte).
- **Format sketch**: header additions when `coder = 1`

  ```
  hypMask     varint   which hypothesis groups are enabled (0 = none: P1 alone)
  oldRegions  varint count, then per region: offLo, length, vaLo   (varints; count 0 = identity)
  newRegions  the same for the new file
  oldBase, newBase   varints (image base, used by rva32)
  ```

  Semantics, computed identically by encoder and decoder from the old file, the control string
  and these tables: the map `FWD` (longest segment first; gaps extrapolated from the preceding
  matched byte, then from the following one), the field shapes of section 4.1, and for each
  active hypothesis at each position the predicted byte and its reliability context
  (hypothesis id, old byte before the field, "prediction differs from old", "already-decoded
  bytes of this field agree"). The predicted byte enters F as an expected flag and V as
  expected bits. `tools/addr.h` is the reference.

  The encoder fills the region tables from ELF/Mach-O/PE headers when it recognises the
  container and leaves them empty otherwise. Nothing on the apply side parses a container, and
  hostile tables can only make predictions wrong, not make the decoder read out of bounds
  (every mapped offset is range-checked).

#### P4. Cost-aware alignment — not prototyped; sections 6.3 and 9.2.

#### P5. Base-relative offset hypotheses — measured on the one same-toolchain Go pair (−8% / −12% of matched-data bytes; sections 4.5 and 9.2).

- **Format sketch**: one more header list when `hypMask` has the bit: `count ≤ 8`, then per
  entry `(oldBase, newBase)` as file offsets. Hypothesis: 4-aligned LE field `v ≠ 0`,
  `v' = FWD[oldBase + v] − newBase`. The encoder may take the bases from the section table,
  from a Go `pcHeader`, or from a voting pass over differing fields; the decoder does not care.

#### P6. Learned target map — oracle only; section 4.2.

- **Sketch**: a hash table old target address → new target address, updated after each
  decoded field that looks like a call or pointer in both files; one more hypothesis that uses
  the table instead of `FWD`. No format change beyond a `hypMask` bit.

### 9.3 Not investigated, or cut short

- **Fixed-point model.** The prototype mixes in `float` and calls `expf`: deterministic for one
  binary, not across platforms. The C# coder must be integer-only and re-measured.
- **Speed on an unthrottled core**, and any optimisation of the prototype.
- **Cost-aware alignment** (section 6.3), **reference-aware matching**, and **P6** in the coder.
- **Block-only mode** patch sizes; sampled suffix arrays; spaced seeds: analysis only.
- **Inferring VA regions and offset bases without a container parser** (voting over differing
  fields): designed, not built.
- **xdelta3, HDiffPatch, detools, Zucchini binaries**: not installed, not benchmarked;
  literature only.
- **The two static-library pairs** were not examined beyond the tables (their fields are
  relocations; a relocation-aware hypothesis was not tried).
- **Compressed containers** (zip, gzip, APK): out of scope.
- **An LZ coder for the extra string whose window holds the old file *and* all of the new file
  decoded so far.** The whole-file LZMA2 result (sections 3.3, 9.2) says this is where the
  remaining bytes of large-change pairs are; P7 is the crude version of it.

### 9.4 Caveats

- One corpus, 20 pairs, three languages. No 32-bit, big-endian, ARM32/Thumb or RISC-V inputs.
- The machine was shared with other agents' builds and benchmarks, and this session ran at
  background priority. Sizes are unaffected; times are CPU seconds and pessimistic.
- A first batch of long runs was stopped on the coordinator's request to free the machine; all
  numbers in this report come from runs that completed, and partial outputs were discarded.
